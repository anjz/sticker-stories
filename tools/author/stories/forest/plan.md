# Forest — story roster

50 stories: 4 fallbacks (no featured stickers) + 46 centred on 3–4 stickers.
Every sticker is featured at least 5 times and is the hero of at least one story.

## Roster

| # | id | featured | supporting | tags | premise | inspiration |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | the-forest-wakes-up | — | tree, flower, bird, butterfly | gentle, morning, fallback | The forest wakes one sound at a time, from the first drip of dew to the sun reaching the tallest branch. | Cumulative waking-song folk pattern (The House That Jack Built), turned into a dawn roll call of sounds: one drop, two, three, a voice, an answer, the wind, the sun. |
| 2 | the-wind-plays-hide-and-seek | — | tree, flower, butterfly, bird | weather, funny, fallback | The wind plays hide-and-seek with the whole forest and nobody can catch it, until everyone stops and listens. | The North Wind and the Sun (Aesop), turned: the wind is a playmate rather than a rival, and stillness, not chasing, is what finds it. |
| 3 | rain-in-three-sizes | — | tree, flower, mushroom, frog | weather, bedtime, counting, fallback | Rain arrives in three sizes, tiny, medium and big, and the forest drinks and hums until the cloud is empty. | Three-sizes folk motif (Goldilocks), turned into weather with a counting refrain the child can say along. |
| 4 | good-night-forest | — | tree, flower, butterfly, owl | bedtime, gentle, fallback | The forest goes to sleep one light at a time, and the moon climbs up to take the night shift. | Lullaby litany (folk goodnight songs that name each thing in turn); the moon as a kind night-watch so the dark is safe. |
| 5 | acorn-wants-to-be-tall | acorn, tree, squirrel | bird | patience, seasons, gentle | Acorn wants to be tall right now; Squirrel buries her, Tree tells her tall takes a while, and in spring a green shoot pokes into the sun. | Kipling Just So how-things-came-to-be, turned: the tallest tree in the forest began as the smallest thing, buried and waiting. |
| 6 | hedgehog-and-the-leaf-coat | hedgehog, squirrel, mouse | tree | funny, kindness, seasons | Hedgehog rolls in leaves to look grand for the leaf parade and ends up a leaf ball; Mouse and Squirrel pluck him out and everyone wears one leaf. | The Jackdaw in Borrowed Feathers (Aesop), turned: nobody laughs at the one who overdressed; friends unpick him and share the costume. |
| 7 | snails-grand-expedition | snail, ladybug, mushroom | flower | adventure, patience, gentle | Snail sets out for Mushroom beyond the old log; Ladybug flies ahead and back three times asking if he is nearly there; Snail arrives at sunset and Mushroom has waited. | The Tortoise and the Hare (Aesop), turned: nobody races, nobody naps; the slow journey is the adventure and the finish line has been waiting all along. |
| 8 | frogs-rain-song | frog, bird, flower | tree | music, weather, funny | The flower is thirsty; Frog sings for rain in her croaky voice; Bird tries to teach her a prettier tune; the rain comes for the croak. | Folk rain-calling songs and La Fontaine's frogs, turned: nobody is punished for singing badly; your own voice turns out to be the right one. |
| 9 | bee-and-the-sleepy-flower | bee, flower, butterfly | ladybug | gentle, morning, kindness | Flower will not open one cloudy morning; fanning and tickling fail; Bee lands softly and hums until the petals open on their own. | The North Wind and the Sun (Aesop): what force cannot open, warmth does; here the warmth is a friend's patient hum. |
| 10 | owl-loses-his-hoot | owl, mouse, deer | tree | funny, kindness, bedtime | Owl wakes up hooting squeaks; Mouse lends his squeak, Deer lends a low hum, and their silly hoot together makes Owl laugh his own hoot back. | The Lion and the Mouse (Aesop) and folk voice-swap tales, turned: the smallest and the tallest lend the wise one their voices, and laughter is the cure. |
| 11 | raccoon-washes-the-moon | raccoon, frog, snail | flower | curiosity, bedtime, funny | Raccoon sees the moon in the pond and tries to wash it clean; Frog and Snail help; the ripples make the moon wobble and everyone giggles until the water goes still. | The Moon in the Well (folk tale of villagers who try to fish the moon out), turned: not fools to laugh at but curious friends, and the joke is one they all share. |
| 12 | pinecone-rolls-away | pinecone, squirrel, hedgehog | tree, mouse | adventure, funny | Pinecone drops from a branch and rolls down the hill, bump bump bump; Squirrel and Hedgehog chase her; at the bottom she has found the sunniest spot in the forest. | The Runaway Pancake (folk chase tale), turned: nobody catches or eats the runaway; the roll is a joyride, and it ends in the best spot of all. |
| 13 | mouse-and-the-big-echo | mouse, deer, rabbit | tree | courage, curiosity, funny | Mouse squeaks hello into the hollow tree and a giant voice answers; Deer and Rabbit come to see; the giant is Mouse's own echo, and he shouts again on purpose. | Folk echo tales (The Dog and the Echo), turned: nothing to fear in the hollow; the biggest voice in the forest turns out to be the smallest friend's own. |
| 14 | deer-and-the-far-away-flower | deer, flower, butterfly, bee | — | gentle, kindness | Deer is so tall and so careful of his hooves that he cannot bend down to smell Flower; Butterfly and Bee carry her scent up to him on their wings. | The Fox and the Grapes (Aesop), turned: instead of deciding the flower is not worth it, the tall one is helped by the small ones, and the flower is glad to be shared. |
| 15 | ladybug-counts-her-spots | ladybug, mouse, snail | flower | counting, funny | Ladybug cannot count her own spots; Mouse counts fast and gets nine, Snail counts slow and gets six; counting together, one by one, they find seven, and a dewdrop pretending. | The Blind Men and the Elephant (folk), turned kind: each friend sees a piece, nobody argues, and counting together adds up. |
| 16 | bird-and-owl-share-the-sky | bird, tree, owl | flower | music, morning, kindness | Bird sings the sun up just as Owl wants to sleep; instead of arguing, they split the sky: Bird sings sunrise, Owl sings sunset, and Tree hums in between. | The Owl and the Nightingale (medieval debate poem), turned: nobody wins the argument; they turn it into a duet with a tree keeping time. |
| 17 | squirrel-forgets-where | squirrel, acorn, raccoon | tree | funny, kindness | Squirrel hides Acorn for later and forgets where; Raccoon helps sniff her out; they dig in three wrong places before Acorn giggles from under the fourth. | Three-tries folk pattern and The Ant and the Grasshopper (Aesop), turned: no scolding for the forgetful one, only a friend with a good nose and a giggling acorn. |
| 18 | mushrooms-umbrella | mushroom, ladybug, bee, butterfly | — | weather, kindness | Rain begins; Mushroom offers her cap as an umbrella to Ladybug, then Bee, then Butterfly, and stretches a little wider each time so everyone stays dry. | The Mitten (Ukrainian folk tale), turned: the shelter grows instead of bursting, and the last arrival is the last drop of rain. |
| 19 | fox-learns-to-tiptoe | fox, deer, mouse | flower | gentle, funny | Fox's big bouncy steps keep waking everyone at nap time; Deer shows him how to lift each hoof slowly, Mouse shows him whisper-paws, and Fox tiptoes so well he surprises himself. | Folk pattern of the loud one taught by the quiet ones (The Elephant and the Mouse motif), turned: Fox is not scolded but shown, and gets to be proud of quiet. |
| 20 | rabbit-walks-with-snail | rabbit, snail, flower | tree | patience, gentle, curiosity | Rabbit hops ahead and back, waiting for Snail; when he finally walks at Snail's pace he sees a dewdrop, a caterpillar and a flower opening that he had always hopped past. | The Tortoise and the Hare (Aesop), turned: there is no race and no loser; slowness has treasures the fast one never noticed. |
| 21 | tree-finds-room-for-everyone | tree, owl, squirrel, bird | — | kindness, bedtime, weather | On a windy night Tree sorts out who sleeps where in his branches, Owl high, Bird middle, Squirrel in the hollow, and everyone fits with a squeeze. | The Mitten (folk) and Andersen's The Fir Tree, turned: the tree does not wish to be elsewhere; being everyone's home is exactly what he wants. |
| 22 | flower-leans-over | flower, mushroom, snail | butterfly | kindness, gentle | Flower grows tallest in the patch and her shadow falls on Mushroom; when Snail points it out, she learns to lean so the sun reaches everyone. | The Oak and the Reed (La Fontaine), turned: bending is not weakness but kindness, and the tall one chooses it. |
| 23 | butterfly-cannot-choose | butterfly, flower, bee, ladybug | — | funny, curiosity | Butterfly cannot decide which flower to land on and flutters over the meadow all day; Bee says just try one; the first one Butterfly tries is perfect. | Buridan's donkey between two haystacks (folk paradox), turned gentle: no one goes hungry, and trying beats choosing. |
| 24 | hedgehog-wants-a-hug | hedgehog, rabbit, raccoon | flower | kindness, funny | Hedgehog wants a hug but his prickles get in the way; Rabbit and Raccoon try a leaf-hug, a tail-hug, and finally a nose-boop that works perfectly. | The Porcupine's Dilemma (Schopenhauer's parable), turned for four-year-olds: there is always a way to be close, and friends will invent it. |
| 25 | owls-slowest-story | owl, mouse, hedgehog, frog | tree | bedtime | Owl tells the three sleepiest friends a story that gets slower and slower, word by word, until it is only hoo... hoo... and everyone is asleep. | The story that never ends (folk endless-tale pattern), turned into a lullaby that winds down instead of looping. |
| 26 | the-great-jump-contest | frog, rabbit, fox | tree | funny, adventure | Rabbit jumps highest, Fox jumps farthest, and Frog jumps straight into the pond with a splash; the contest becomes about the best landing, and everybody jumps in. | The Frog Who Wished to Be as Big as the Ox (La Fontaine), turned: Frog does not try to be bigger; she wins by being exactly a frog, and the others join her. |
| 27 | the-tiny-picnic | bee, ladybug, pinecone, acorn | mouse, squirrel | kindness, counting | Four tiny friends each bring one tiny thing to a picnic, a drop of honey, a crumb, a seed and a petal cup, and together it is a feast on a pinecone table. | Stone Soup (folk), turned tiny: no trickery needed, everyone brings a little and it adds up to plenty. |
| 28 | raccoons-shiny-treasure | raccoon, ladybug, bird | flower | curiosity, kindness | Raccoon finds a dewdrop shining like treasure and tries to carry it home, but it keeps slipping away; Bird shows them the sunshine inside it belongs to everyone. | The Dog and His Reflection (Aesop), turned: not greed but wonder, and the treasure is not lost, only shared. |
| 29 | deer-and-the-quiet-game | deer, fox, rabbit, frog | — | funny, gentle | Deer is the champion of the quiet game; Fox giggles, Rabbit's foot thumps, Frog croaks, and just when Deer has won, he sneezes, and nobody minds losing to a sneeze. | The Silent Couple (folk tale of a contest of silence), turned into a game of giggles where losing is the fun part. |
| 30 | mouse-climbs-the-tree | mouse, tree, acorn | squirrel | courage, adventure | Mouse wants to see what the top of Tree looks like; he climbs branch by branch, resting on each one with Acorn for company, and reaches the top just as the sun sets. | Perseverance folk tales (the little engine pattern), turned: resting on every branch counts as climbing, and the view is shared. |
| 31 | pinecones-secret | pinecone, squirrel, bird | mouse, tree | curiosity, seasons | On a wet day Pinecone keeps her scales shut tight and Squirrel and Bird wonder what she is hiding; when the sun comes out she opens, and her secret flies away on tiny wings. | Kipling Just So wonder at a real thing (pinecones open in dry air and release winged seeds), turned: patience, not prying, reveals the secret. |
| 32 | ladybugs-leaf-boat | ladybug, frog, snail | flower | adventure, weather | After the rain a puddle is as wide as a sea; Ladybug captains a leaf boat across it, Frog paddles with her big feet, and Snail is the anchor when the wind blows. | Nonsense voyage poems (Lear's The Owl and the Pussy-cat), turned into a puddle crossing where the slow one holds the boat steady. |
| 33 | fox-chases-his-tail | fox, butterfly, hedgehog | flower | funny | Fox spots something fluffy behind him and chases it round and round; Butterfly lands on it and it stops; Hedgehog explains it has been following him all along, because it is his tail. | Aesop's fox-and-tail motifs and the folk dog-chases-tail joke, turned: the silly chase ends in a laugh Fox shares and a dizzy nap. |
| 34 | rabbit-loses-his-bounce | rabbit, frog, bee | flower | kindness, gentle | Rabbit wakes up flat and bounceless; Frog tries jumping for him and Bee tries buzzing him up; in the end they just sit with him and hum, and his hop comes back on its own. | Andersen's kindness tales, turned: slow days are allowed, nobody is fixed by force, and friends who simply stay are the cure. |

| 39 | owl-tries-the-daytime | owl, bee, squirrel, rabbit | — | curiosity, funny | Owl stays awake to find out what daytime is like; it is bright and busy and noisy; Bee, Squirrel and Rabbit each show him one lovely day thing before he falls asleep smiling. | Andersen curiosity tales and folk owl-and-sun motifs, turned: the night bird is not mocked for the daytime; his friends make it worth seeing. |
