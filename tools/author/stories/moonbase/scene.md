# Moon Base — the scene (what the child sees behind the stickers)

Written 2026-09-27 from the pack's art (`packs/moonbase/art/background*.webp`
and `foreground*.webp`, viewed with a grid). Every story plays over this
picture, so the words may only put a story in places the picture has.
Positions are fractions of the base 4:3 picture (what an iPad shows),
measured from the **left** and from the **top**; the wide picture (phones
in landscape) adds more of the same plain, ridge and rocks at both sides.
Wide screens crop a band off the top and the bottom.

It is our own Moon, in daylight: a world with no air, so the sky is black
even by day, nothing is blown about, and no sound carries across the
plain.

## What is not there

No Sun painted (the light comes from off the picture), no clouds, no air,
no wind, no rain, no water to see (except the ice sticker); no road,
track or path, no tyre tracks or footprints painted (a story may make
footprints, said in passing); no hill near us, no cave, tunnel or hole
to go into; no trees, grass or plants outside (plants grow in the
greenhouse and the seedling's pod, both stickers); nothing inside the
habitat can be seen; no fence, flag or sign; no second crater big
enough to drive into. The domes far off are too far to walk to.

## What the canvas effects can and cannot change

The effects add light and specks over the painting; they never repaint
it.

- `nightfall`: the whole scene darkens to a deep blue-grey and more stars
  come out, steady (no twinkling: no air). The Earth stays as painted —
  say "the Earth glowed", never "the Earth went out". It lasts until
  `daybreak` or the end.
- `daybreak`: stark white sunlight sweeps across from the left and a
  soft glare sits in the top-left corner. No golden dawn, no birdsong.
- `eclipse`: the light turns a dim copper-red for a while. The Earth
  stays painted as it is: say "the light turned red", not "the Earth
  went black".
- `milkyway`: a pale band of stars across the top of the sky.
- `satellites`: a few tiny lights glide slowly across the sky.
- `comet`: a comet with a glowing tail glides across the upper sky.
- `dust`: grey dust billows up across the lower part of the plain and
  falls back.
- `floodlights`: two white beams from the top corners and pools of light
  on the ground.
- `glints`: soft sparkles on the ground here and there.

None of them adds a building, a track, water, snow or a new crater.

## How stories use it

- Put the story where the picture is: on the plain, on the landing pad,
  at (or in) the crater, at the foot of the habitat's ladder, at the foot
  of the radio mast, in the sky above the ridge, looking at the Earth.
- Inside the habitat is off stage: "went in through the hatch" and "came
  out again" are fine in passing; the story never happens inside.
- Things a story makes or finds (a brick wall, a footprint, a message)
  never appear on screen: keep them small and brief.
- The spacecraft are far overhead in the sky or land on the pad; they
  never come down to stand beside the crew on the plain (except the
  lander, the rocket and the supply pod, which land on the pad).
- Sounds: the Moon has no air, so the soundtrack is what the crew hear
  in their helmets (the radio, their own breathing, a hum through their
  boots) or a storyteller's music — a story about silence plays no clang.
