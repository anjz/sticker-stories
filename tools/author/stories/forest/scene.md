# Forest — the scene (what the child sees behind the stickers)

Written 2026-09-25 from the pack's art (`packs/forest/art/background*.webp`
and `foreground*.webp`, viewed with a grid). Every story plays over this
picture, so the words may only put a story in places the picture has.
Positions are fractions of the base 4:3 picture (what an iPad shows),
measured from the **left** and from the **top**; the wide picture (phones
in landscape) adds more of the same meadow, trees and bushes at both
sides. Wide screens crop a band off the top and the bottom, so the tree
tops and the bottom row of plants may be cut.

## What is not there

No near hill, slope, bank or hilltop (only the faint far ridge); no stream,
brook, river, waterfall, lake or sea; no path, road or bridge; no log,
stump, hollow, hole, burrow, den, cave, nest or house; no fence, garden or
field of crops; no mud or puddles (rain does not add them); no reeds or
cattails at the pond; no mushrooms, apples, nuts or pinecones except the
stickers; no snow on the ground, no autumn colours on the trees.

## What the canvas effects can and cannot change

The effects add light, weather and particles over the painting; they never
repaint it. `snow` makes flakes fall and the top of the sky white, but the
meadow and the trees stay green — say "snowflakes fell", not "everything
was white". `leaves` makes red and gold leaves flutter down, but the two
trees stay green — say "leaves came twirling down", not "the trees turned
gold". `night` darkens everything and puts a moon in the sky; `sunset`
turns the light golden-pink; `sunshine` brightens it with rays; `rain`,
`fog`, `clouds`, `wind`, `fireflies`, `rainbow`, `confetti`, `bubbles` are
what they say. None of them adds a path, puddles, a nest or a snowman.

## How stories use it

- Put the story where the picture is: on the meadow, by (or in) the
  pond, under or up in the two big trees, among the far bushes, by a
  rock, among the flowers. The sticker stages already land there.
- A place the picture lacks can only be **off stage** and never the scene
  of the action: "on his way home", "a nest high in the tree" said in
  passing is fine; "Mouse couldn't cross the stream", "Pinecone rolled down
  the hill" are not — the child is looking at a flat meadow with a pond.
- Things a story makes or finds (a den Beaver builds, a nest, a leaf pile,
  a ring of mushrooms) never appear on screen: keep them small and brief,
  or tell them so the words do not ask the child to look at them.
- Sounds may come from off stage ("far away, another woodpecker drummed
  back"), but not from scenery that should be visible ("a brook bubbled"
  suggests water that is not there).
