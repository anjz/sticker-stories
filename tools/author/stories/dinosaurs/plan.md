# Dinosaurs — story roster

2026-10-30. 50 stories: 4 fallbacks (no featured stickers) + 46 centred
on 3–4 stickers. Every sticker is featured at least 5 times and is the
hero of at least one story. Setting `outdoors`: canvas effects wherever
the words describe weather or light. 20 stories (40 %) carry one small
true fact. Two writers: **rows 1–25 writer A, rows 26–50 writer B**.

**Audience: 4–8** (picture-book, like Forest and Beach). 100–150 words a
language (50–80 s), every safety rule in GUIDE.md. The scene is
`scene.md`: the lagoon (with four giant lily pads), its sandy-mud shore
with pebbles and grey boulders, the fern meadow, the big flat terracotta
rock on the right, the tree fern (left) and the monkey-puzzle tree
(right) whose low branches are perches, the far red cliffs and the sleepy
old mountain far away. Narrator only: no character voices (no
`{x:says}`); dialogue in quotes in English, with the raya in Spanish.

## Pack rules (the user's, for this pack and all packs)

- **Nothing scary.** No predators, no hunting, no eating other animals,
  no fights, no chase that ends in a catch, **no teeth** (never the word
  — also no *bite*, *claws*, *sharp*, *hunt*, *prey*). T. rex is young,
  round and friendly: his roar is a tiny squeak, he never eats anything
  in a story and never frightens anyone.
- **The volcano never erupts, smokes, rumbles or glows.** In the words
  it is only *the sleepy old mountain far away* / *la vieja montaña
  dormida, allá lejos* — never the word volcano / volcán.
- **No people.** No eggs-for-breakfast, no "extinct", no "millions of
  years ago", no fossils of anyone.
- **Plant-eaters eat plants** (ferns, leaves, seeds, berries) — never a
  plant that is a character: nobody munches Fern, Flower or the big
  Leaf; Berry Bush's berries only when Bush offers them.
- **Mixing ages is fine, claiming it is not**: Brachiosaurus and
  Stegosaurus (Jurassic) share the valley with T. rex and Triceratops
  (Cretaceous) in the stories, but no story ever says they lived at the
  same time. Facts are true of the species they are told about.
- **Not every animal is a dinosaur**: Pteranodon (a flying reptile),
  Plesiosaur (a sea reptile), Ammonite, Turtle, Dragonfly, Beetle and
  Glider (a little furry animal) are never called dinosaurs. Words for
  everyone: *everyone*, *the whole valley*, *all the friends*.
- **Objects never act by themselves**: the log, the leaf, the puddle and
  the eggs' nest move only when the wind, the rain or a character moves
  them (a stomp rocks the log, rain plops into the puddle, raindrops
  bounce the leaf). The babies inside the eggs are alive: tapping,
  wobbling, peeking and hatching are theirs.
- **Grow stickers are there from the start**: `fern`, `flower`, `bush`,
  `log`, `leaf`, `puddle` and `eggs` enter (`{x:enter}`) before the
  first word of the text, unless the story makes them appear.
- **Stage follows the words**: every place the words put a character, a
  move takes it there (`go to lagoon`, `go to rocks`, `go to branches`,
  `go on log`, `go under leaf`…), timed to the words; every two
  characters the words put together, a move brings one to the other.
- **No long pauses** (no `[long pause]`); one or two `…` or `[short
  pause]` a story at most.
- **Real transcreation**: Spanish (es-ES) written natively from the
  beats; Spanish sound words (*plaf*, *chof*, *toc, toc*, *ñam*, *fiu*,
  *bluf*), no calques.

Forbidden by storycheck in English: *shut*, *hit*, *fat*, *fight*,
*hurt*, *dead*… (say "closed her eyes", "bumped"); in Spanish *golpe*,
*pegar*, *tonto*… Weather words are canvas cues: *wind/gust*, *rain*,
*cloud(s)*, *moon*, *fog/mist*, *party*, *bubbles* / *viento*,
*lluvia*, *nube(s)*, *luna*, *niebla*, *fiesta*, *burbujas* — cue the
effect or reword ("a breeze", "a bubble… and another", "burbujitas").
Place words (feature `words`): *branch, lagoon, lake, shore, bank, rock,
ledge, meadow, ferns* / *rama, laguna, lago, orilla, roca, repisa,
prado, helechos* — a character in that sentence needs a move there.
